Electrician invoice template

Electrical jobs mix labor, materials, and often permits — three things that belong on their own lines. This electrician invoice template separates them so the invoice is easy for a homeowner or a general contractor to approve.

What to put on a electrician invoice

  • Service call / trip charge
  • Labor — hours × rate, per electrician
  • Materials — wire, breakers, fixtures, itemized
  • Permit and inspection fees
  • Payment terms and due date

Common questions

What should an electrician’s invoice include?

Labor, materials, and any permit or inspection fees on separate lines, plus the service-call charge if you have one. Itemize materials with quantities so the charge is verifiable.

Do I need to show permit fees?

If the job required a permit, yes — list the fee as its own line. Clients accept a pass-through fee much more readily than a lump sum that hides it.

Should I itemize materials or quote a flat rate?

Both are legitimate. If you quote flat-rate, still show the labor and materials split so the customer understands the value; if time-and-materials, itemize everything.
