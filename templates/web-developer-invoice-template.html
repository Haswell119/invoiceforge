Web projects blur the line between the build, the setup, and the ongoing maintenance — three things a client will happily question as one lump sum. This web developer invoice template separates the build, hosting, and recurring care so each is explicit.

What to put on a web developer invoice

  • Project build — fixed price or hours × rate
  • Milestone payments and the amount already paid
  • Hosting, domain, and third-party setup
  • Recurring maintenance or retainer
  • Payment terms and due date

Common questions

How do web developers invoice?

Bill the build per milestone, then itemize hosting, domain, and any third-party costs separately. Recurring maintenance is its own line or a separate invoice.

Should I bill by milestone?

On projects over a few thousand, yes — invoice on milestone completion and show the amount already paid so the client always sees the outstanding balance.

Do I pass through hosting costs?

List hosting and domain as pass-through items at cost, or fold them into a maintenance retainer. Never bury them in the build total.
