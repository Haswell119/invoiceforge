How to invoice as a consultant

Hourly, day rate or fixed fee — and what belongs on a consulting invoice.

Consulting invoices look like any other invoice, with one difference: the client is usually buying your judgement, not a box of parts. That changes how you describe the work, how you price it, and how much detail accounts payable needs before they release the money.

Pick a billing model and stay consistent

Most consultants bill in one of four ways. The model decides what your line items look like, so choose it before you write the first invoice.

ModelLine item showsGood when
HourlyHours × rate, often grouped by week or phaseScope is genuinely unknown and the client controls pace
Day rateDays × rate (state your day length, e.g. 8 hours)On-site or workshop-heavy engagements
Fixed fee / milestoneNamed deliverable at a stated price, invoiced per milestoneScope is agreed and you carry the risk
RetainerMonthly fee for an agreed availability or volumeOngoing advisory work

Whatever you pick, put the same wording on every invoice. A client who sees "Strategy retainer — March" three months running approves it without reading; a client who sees a new description each month asks questions.

What goes on a consulting invoice

  • Invoice number and dates. One continuous sequence, an issue date and a due date. See how to choose an invoice number format.
  • Your trading name, address, email and tax number if you are registered for VAT/GST or sales tax.
  • The client's legal entity and its purchase-order number. Many companies cannot pay an invoice that is missing the PO number — get it in writing before you start.
  • Work period. "Consulting services, 1–31 March 2026" removes any doubt about which month is being billed.
  • Line items. A short description, quantity (hours, days or 1), unit rate, and line total.
  • Pre-approved expenses as their own lines, usually at cost, with a receipt attached if the contract requires it.
  • Subtotal, tax, total, currency — tax shown as a separate line, never folded into the total.
  • Payment instructions. Bank details or a payment link, plus the reference the client must quote.

Describe the value, not just the hours

"Consulting — 12 hours" invites the question "doing what?". A line that reads "Discovery interviews and pricing analysis (deliverable: 18-page findings memo)" gets approved faster because it points at something the client already received. Keep descriptions under a dozen words and lead with the deliverable.

Deposits, retainers and milestone billing

Cash flow is the main risk in consulting, and the way to manage it is to invoice earlier. Three arrangements work well:

  1. Deposit up front. 30–50% on signature, balance on delivery. Label it clearly — "Deposit (50%) against engagement fee".
  2. Milestone invoicing. Break a fixed fee into two or three milestones and invoice each as it completes. Never leave 100% of the fee until the end.
  3. Retainer in advance. Invoice the monthly retainer on the 1st, due on receipt or net 7. Retainers invoiced in arrears are an interest-free loan to your client.

Payment terms that hold

Net 14 or net 30 is the usual range, and it is worth knowing what net 30 actually commits you to. Two extra clauses save a lot of chasing: a late fee, and a statement that work stops if an invoice goes unpaid. The late fee calculator shows what a given rate works out to on a specific invoice.

When a payment does run late, send a short factual reminder rather than a long complaint. We keep a set of payment reminder email templates for exactly that.

Mistakes that delay consultant payments

  • Missing PO number — the single most common reason a consultant invoice sits in a queue.
  • Invoicing the wrong entity. Bill the company that signed the contract, not the brand or the subsidiary you worked with day to day.
  • Vague line items. "Advisory services — $8,000" gets queried; three named deliverables do not.
  • No due date. Undated invoices drift.
  • Billing in arrears for a retainer. You are funding the client's working capital.
  • Unapproved expenses. An expense line the client did not agree to in advance becomes a negotiation.

Keep the paperwork boring

Consulting invoices do not need to look impressive, they need to be unambiguous. Fixed format, fixed numbering, fixed terms: the invoice stops being a task and becomes a two-minute habit that gets you paid on schedule.
