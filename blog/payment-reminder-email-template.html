Payment reminder email templates for overdue invoices

Four emails, in order. Short, factual, and escalating exactly once per step.

Chasing money is the least enjoyable part of freelancing, and most of the discomfort comes from not knowing what to write. The templates below cover the whole sequence: a nudge before the due date, a reminder on the day, a firm first reminder, and a final notice. Replace everything in square brackets and send.

The rules that make reminders work

  • Write to a person. Use the name of whoever approves or processes the invoice. “Hi accounts” gets filed; a named recipient replies.
  • Lead with the facts. Invoice number, amount, due date. In that order, in the first two lines.
  • One ask per email. Either “is there anything blocking payment?” or “please pay by Friday” — not both.
  • Never apologise for invoicing. You delivered the work; the invoice is the agreed next step.
  • Attach the invoice again. It costs nothing and removes the “we never received it” path.
  • Keep a log. Date sent, date of each reminder, date paid. If a dispute ever escalates, the log is your evidence.

1. Courtesy nudge (2–3 days before the due date)

Subject: Invoice [#INV-1042] — due [Friday 27 March]

Hi [Name],

A quick heads-up that invoice [INV-1042] for [$2,500] is due on [Friday 27 March].

Nothing is needed from you if it is already scheduled — I am only flagging it in case it needs to go through a purchase-order or approval step first. The invoice is attached.

Thanks,
[Your name]

2. Due today

Subject: Invoice [#INV-1042] is due today

Hi [Name],

Invoice [INV-1042] for [$2,500] is due today, [27 March].

Payment details are on the invoice. If it has already been processed, please ignore this note and thank you.

Best,
[Your name]

3. First reminder (1–3 days overdue)

Subject: Overdue: invoice [#INV-1042] — [$2,500]

Hi [Name],

Invoice [INV-1042] for [$2,500] was due on [27 March] and I have not yet seen the payment.

Is there anything on my side holding it up — a missing reference, a PO number, or a different billing contact? Happy to reissue it in whatever format your system needs.

If it has already been scheduled, a payment date would be helpful.

Thanks,
[Your name]

4. Final notice (7–14 days overdue)

Subject: Second reminder: invoice [#INV-1042] — [$2,500], now [14] days overdue

Hi [Name],

Invoice [INV-1042] for [$2,500], due [27 March], is now [14] days overdue.

Under our agreement, overdue amounts accrue interest at [X]% per annum from the due date, and work pauses on accounts more than [30] days overdue. I would rather not apply either — please let me know today when the payment will be released, or tell me what is blocking it and I will fix it.

Invoice attached again for convenience.

Regards,
[Your name]

Only mention the late fee and the work pause if they are genuinely in your contract. An empty threat costs you the next piece of work; a real clause costs the client money, which is why it works.

What to do when the reminder is ignored

Escalate once more, in writing, to the named person and the person who signed the contract. Then decide: keep working, stop working, or hand the debt to a collection route. Before you do any of that, check whether the invoice can be fixed — a missing PO number or the wrong legal entity is behind a surprising share of “late” payments. Stop the next one from being late

Reminders are the recovery path; the prevention is upstream. Shorter terms, an explicit due date, a named recipient and a PO number captured before work starts will remove most of the emails above from your calendar entirely.
