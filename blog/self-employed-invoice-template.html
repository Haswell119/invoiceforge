Self-employed invoice template: the fields that actually get you paid

A self-employed invoice is simpler than a company invoice — but two wording mistakes make clients park it. Here is exactly what to put on one.

If you trade under your own name rather than a company, your invoice has to do a little more work than a business one: it has to make clear who is being paid and why no one should be confused by the lack of a company number. This is a practical list of what belongs on a self-employed invoice, and a free way to produce one without downloading a template.

The eight fields a self-employed invoice needs

  1. Your full name and address — your own legal name, not just a trading name. "J. Smith, Freelance Design" is fine as a brand, but the payee line should be the name on your bank account, or the payment stalls.
  2. A contact for payment questions — an email or phone. A client with a query about your invoice should never have to dig for how to reach you.
  3. A unique invoice number — sequential, never reused, so both of you can find it again. How to choose a numbering scheme.
  4. Issue date and a specific due date — "Due 14 March", not "Net 30". A real date is the only thing an accounts person can put straight into a payment run.
  5. A clear description of the work — line items with quantity and rate. "Landing page design, 1, £350" gets paid; "work as agreed" does not.
  6. Subtotal, tax and total, stated separately — with the tax rate shown. If you are not VAT/GST registered, say so on the document rather than leaving the tax line blank.
  7. Your payment details — bank account, sort code/routing number, and currency. Terms written on the invoice are read; terms that live only in your contract are not.
  8. A due date, not a hope — the single highest-leverage field. Invoices with a specific date are paid measurably faster than "within 30 days".

What is different when you are self-employed

A company invoice carries a registered company name and number; a self-employed invoice carries you. Three practical consequences:

  • Use your own name as the payee. If the name on the invoice does not match the name on the bank account, payment gets held for "name mismatch" — the single most common reason a self-employed invoice is delayed.
  • No company number. Do not leave a "company registration number" field blank in a way that looks unfinished. If your template shows one, remove the field entirely rather than leaving a gap.
  • State your tax position. If you are not registered for VAT/GST, a one-line note — "Not VAT registered" — prevents the client from assuming tax should have been added or withheld.
